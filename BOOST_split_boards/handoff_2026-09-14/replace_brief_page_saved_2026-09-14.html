Before placing

Decide these first

Each of these changes a footprint, a keep-out or a part height. Placing before they’re settled means placing twice.

D1

Switching edges and FET package

Open

Every commutation loop runs through three TO-220 packages: M1, the LX-side FET and the rail FET’s body diode. At 8 A/ns the copper alone puts 74 / 137 / 99 V on LX for channels 1 / 2 / 3, and the audit puts the loop with package leads above 350 V. The 70 V target allows 4.3 nH in the whole loop.

ChangesFET footprints · underside layout · tab keep-outs · stack height

D2

Current-sense connections

Open

U25 taps the 20.7 A Vin and rsense_lo pours 41 mm from R1, so it reads 32 % high and RESET trips at 30.6 A instead of 40.5 A. Each sink op-amp senses 42–270 mΩ of track upstream of its shunt, so the LEDs get 45–84 % less current than set.

Changesschematic nets · shunt footprints · where U25, U6, U11 and U12 go

Either wayPut U25 beside R1 and each sink op-amp beside its shunt.

D3

Copper weight

Open

Neither board declares a stackup. The gerber job says 1 oz on every layer, and a JLCPCB order that doesn’t say otherwise gets 1 oz outer / 0.5 oz inner. Every width in the re-route follows from this.

D4

Board-to-board link, J10 / J11

Open

J10 and J11 are both male and mirrored, so they can’t mate. The ten analog pins (21–30) have no ground among them, the feedback dividers are split across the link, and neither connector is in the schematic.

Changesconnector footprints · pinout · where the control card can sit

Either wayAdd J10/J11 to the schematic, give every analog pin an adjacent GND, and keep both resistors of each divider on the same board.

D5

Control card size and fixing

Open

No 60 × 60 mm card inside the 78 × 90 mm case can avoid L1, which is 19 mm tall, so the stack is 36.5 mm against 33 mm. The card also has no mounting holes.

Changescontrol card outline · keep-outs on the power board · standoffs

Either wayAdd mounting holes and standoffs, and confirm the 220 µF can height: Panasonic lists EEH-ZU1H221P at 16.5 mm, but the footprint name says 10.5 mm.

After placement

Route and check, in order

Don’t start a step until the one before it passes.

  1. Plot both sides and re-measure every distance above against its target.

  2. Move Net-(M8-S), Net-(M9-S) and Net-(M10-S) into the Power class. Add a custom rule for minimum via hole and track width on Power nets, so the router can’t fall back to 0.25 mm vias. Widths come from D3.

    Needs D3

  3. Draw pours and wide tracks path by path, then solve each branch: neck rise ≤ 20 °C (IPC-2221) and every via within its rating (Ø0.40 ≤ 0.97 A, Ø0.25 ≤ 0.71 A).

  4. Autoroute signals only once the power copper is fixed, so no signal track can split a pour.

  5. DRC with 0 errors and 0 unconnected under the 1.0 / 0.15 mm silk rule; no untied copper islands; In1 and In6 carry only GND; no LX on In3 or In5; solver table re-run.

About the numbers

  • “Now” is the straight-line pad-to-pad distance on the current BOOST_power.kicad_pcb. The copper path is always longer.
  • Targets are layout goals for this re-place, not audit thresholds.
  • Track widths use IPC-2221 for a long conductor at a 20 °C rise, so short necks are conservative.
  • The D1 edge rates use the audit’s typical TO-220 lead inductance (4.5 + 7.5 nH per package) and a 35.5 V base. HYG180N10’s datasheet doesn’t state lead inductance.
