## 2.1.1 — 4.0 mm tall-part clearance at tab screws: **accepted**

Conditions:

- **The screws are M3 socket head, driven with a plain 2.5 mm hex shaft** (about 4 mm across, or a
  ball driver about 4.5 mm). That fits the 8 mm channel with room to spare, even past the 16.8 mm
  cans at 4.01 mm. Do **not** assume a 1/4 in bit holder or a nut driver — those are 10–13 mm across
  and will not fit; the build will use a plain-shaft driver.
- Keep the 3.75 mm washer keep-out as it is. An M3 socket head is Ø5.5 mm and a 7 mm washer is
  Ø7 mm, so 3.75 mm is the binding constraint, not 4.0 mm.
- Standoffs stay at 5.5 mm, as you have them.
- Note in the build docs that **M8's and M9's screws go in before the control card**, and that the
  power board is screwed down before the card is fitted.

## 2.1.2 — Standoffs anywhere inside their own card quadrant: **accepted**

The spread you have (20.6 × 32.5 mm, overhang 8.6 / 15.8 / 5.7 / 6.0 mm, J10 down the middle) is
fine for a 1.6 mm card carrying only light parts. No further constraint.

## 2.1.3 — `p15_b10_k1` as the base for copper: **accepted** — but the goal list is measuring the wrong thing

Accept the layout. The three flagged "hard misses" are the least important numbers on the board, and
the number that actually sets M1's turn-off spike is not in the goal list at all.

**Why the cap misses don't matter much.** Every rail FET already has its ceramic pair within 9 mm,
and those carry the switching edge; the 220 µF cans are bulk storage (ESL of a 10 × 16.5 mm can is
about 10 nH, so at 3.8 A/ns they contribute almost nothing either way):

| Channel | rail FET → 4.7 µF 1210 | → 100 nF 0805 | 220 µF cans |
|---|---|---|---|
| ch1 M2 | C1 3.8 mm | C72 5.5 mm | C88 9.0, C86 11.8, C70 20.1 |
| ch2 M3 | C73 8.5 mm | C3 5.3 mm | C78 14.5, C71 15.0, C87 18.8 |
| ch3 M4 | C4 4.8 mm | C76 7.6 mm | C74 8.1, C77 13.7, C75 15.0 |

Each rail also has at least two cans inside 15 mm. C70 at 20.1 mm and C87 at 18.8 mm are acceptable.

**What does matter: the LX segment between M1's drain and the channel's LX FET.** At M1 turn-off the
inductor current jumps into that path, and it is the loop the whole lead-inductance study was about
(`SIMULATION_REPORT.md`). Measured on your board:

| | ch1 (M7) | ch2 (M6) | ch3 (M5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 drain → LX FET drain | **10.9 mm** | **33.1 mm** | **41.9 mm** |
| + shared source | 2.5 | 2.5 | 2.5 |
| + rail FET drain → nearest ceramic | 3.8 | 5.3 | 4.8 |
| forward path, M1 drain → decoupling | **17.2 mm** | **40.9 mm** | **49.2 mm** |
| copper inductance assumed in the sims | 4.85 nH | 12.74 nH | 7.91 nH |

The simulated 68 V peak at M1's die (100 V avalanche, 100 V part) came out of those assumed
inductances. ch3's path is now the longest of the three, and it was simulated with the *smallest*
inductance, so that result is not yet covering the real board.

So:

1. **If a local polish is cheap, spend it on M1 drain → M5 and → M6, not on the three flagged
   misses.** Even 5–10 mm helps. If it costs another few hundred annealing runs, skip it and handle
   it in copper — do not trade away anything that is currently met.
2. **At routing, treat these three paths as the first-class nets:** widest practical LX pour, an
   uninterrupted GND plane directly under it, and a via field (not one or two vias) at every layer
   transition between the B.Cu FETs and the F.Cu caps. The ceramic pairs get their own vias to the
   plane, at least two each.
3. **When the copper is drawn, extract the loop inductance per channel** (M1 drain → LX FET → rail
   FET → nearest ceramic → GND plane → M1 source) and send me the three numbers. I will re-run the
   turn-off sim with the real values. If ch2/ch3 land above about 13 nH, we have levers left
   (raise R15 above 10 Ω, or add a small RC snubber at LX), so this is not a placement blocker.
4. `L1 LX pad → M1 drain 16.5 mm` — **drop this goal, it is not worth layout effort.** L1's own
   6.8 µH dominates that branch; its current cannot change during the switching edge, so that length
   is not in the fast loop. The pour just needs to be wide enough for 40 A peaks.
5. `M9 drain → J7 24.4 mm` is fine — it is a DC path, just give it IPC width.
6. The op-amp misses (U12 OUT → M9 gate 14.7, U6 OUT → M8 gate 15.3, U6 IN− → NT3 11.7) are
   acceptable **provided** the 100 Ω gate resistor (R50/R22) sits at the FET's gate pin, not at the
   op-amp, and the gate and sense traces run over an uninterrupted GND plane. These are linear
   servos; a long gate trace with the resistor at the wrong end invites oscillation.

**M2–M7 at up to 25 mm from a screw: confirmed.** They dissipate well under a watt each (conduction
only, and each pair conducts for roughly a tenth of the cycle) against M1's 4.85 W and the sinks'
2.8–3.2 W, so their contact pressure is not thermally critical.

### D19: `TVS_5KP54A` vs the `D_SMC` footprint
You are right that 5KP is an axial P600 part. **Use an SMC part and keep the footprint: SMCJ54A**
(1500 W, standoff 54 V, VBR 60–66.3 V, VC 87.1 V at 17.2 A, DO-214AB). Change the schematic value;
check LCSC/JLC stock and tell me the part you settle on.

Reasoning: D19 sits across M1's drain and source and its job is to hold the die under 100 V. With
R15 = 10 Ω the sims show no avalanche, a 68 V die peak and only 38 mW average in the TVS, so 1500 W
is not the limiting factor — but an axial part's leads would add several nH in exactly the loop we
are trying to shrink, and you have just placed D19 5.2 / 5.1 mm from M1's pins, which an axial P600
body would ruin. If you want a 5 kW part for battery-side surges, that belongs at J1/J2, not on LX.

### R1 land pattern
I pulled Bourns' datasheet and extracted the text — both are in this folder:
`R1_CSS4J-4026_datasheet.pdf` and `R1_CSS4J-4026_datasheet_text.txt` (REV 01/26). It does have a
"Recommended Pad Layout" figure. Numbers carried in that figure and the product drawing:

- Body: **10.06 ± 0.25 long, 6.60 +0.30/−0.20 wide**; height **H max 2.93 mm for the K-2L00**
  (so R1 counts as a short part, under your 3 mm threshold).
- Terminal/sense features: 2.00 ± 0.12, 1.00 ± 0.15, 0.70 ± 0.10, 0.42 ± 0.10, sense terminals
  **0.90 wide**.
- Recommended pad layout figure: **10.60** overall, **5.60** / **5.50**, **0.90**, **0.80**;
  7.30, 8.90, 6.90 REF and 4.70 REF appear on the drawing.
- 4.45 / 3.80 / 2.45 belong to the "Recommended Measurements" figure (where to put force and sense
  probes), not to the land pattern — worth checking you did not read those as pad dimensions.

Your footprint's sense pads (0.9 mm) match the datasheet. Its force pads are bigger than Bourns'
recommended land: 5.0 × 5.6 mm at ±4.7 mm gives a **14.4 mm** span against the recommended 10.60 mm.
Keep the larger pads — it is a 4 W part and the copper is its heatsink — but:

- set the **paste aperture to the datasheet land**, not the full pad, or the part will float and tilt
  on reflow (it is a 10 mm slug);
- keep a solder-mask dam of at least 0.3 mm between each sense pad and its force pad;
- the sense stub length is still not dimensioned, so make the sense pads slightly *shorter* than the
  terminal rather than longer, and have the user measure a real part before the boards are ordered.

### J10/J11 drill
**Use 1.05 mm.** Samtec's 1.02 ± 0.03 mm is a finished hole; JLCPCB's PTH tolerance is about
±0.08 mm, so a 1.00 mm nominal can finish at 0.92 mm against a 0.64 mm square post whose diagonal is
0.90 mm. At 2.54 mm pitch there is room for a 1.7 mm pad, which still clears the 0.254 mm annular
ring. These are hand-soldered, 60 holes; do not make them tight.

### Sink op-amp accuracy
**Keep the MCP6241.** ±5 mV against the 131 mV shunt signal is ±3.8 %, but **each channel has its own
MCP4451 pot**, 15.5 mA per step (0.6 % of 2.63 A), so a fixed offset is calibrated out at bring-up
and stored. What is left is drift, a few µV/°C, i.e. a fraction of a percent over the whole
temperature range. A zero-drift part would also change the servo loop's bandwidth and force a
stability re-check for no useful gain.

### MCP4451 resistance variant
**10 kΩ — MCP4451-103E/ST.** This is not a free choice: the pot's A terminal is at GND and its B
terminal hangs on the R29/R54 divider (22 k from IREF1, 1 k to GND), so R_AB loads that divider and
sets the full-scale setpoint. `BOOST_CONTEXT_TRANSFER.md` line 150 states the design's full scale as
5 × (1k‖10k)/(22k + 1k‖10k) = **0.198 V → 3.96 A, 15.5 mA/step**, which is the 10 kΩ case exactly.
5 kΩ would drop full scale to 0.182 V, 50 kΩ would raise it to 0.213 V, 100 kΩ to 0.215 V, and every
current calculation in the docs would move with it. With 10 kΩ the wiper's source impedance is at
most 2.5 k + 0.9 k, so the 1 nF C41/C44/C45 give about 3.4 µs — fine.
