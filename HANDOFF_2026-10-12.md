## 1. What the project is

### 1.1 The product
A **three-channel, roughly 100 W-per-channel LED driver** ("BOOST"), designed by the user (GitHub account
ChristopherCa55). It is built into a small **aluminium case that is operated underwater**; the case is the heatsink.
- The early fit study (`ROOT\BOOST_FIT_STUDY.md`) used a 33 mm tall interior, 78 x 90 mm in plan.
- The FETs are screwed to the case floor through thermal pads.
- It drives three high-power LEDs, one per channel:
  - channel 1, "red": about 23.3 V at 2.635 A;
  - channels 2 and 3, "green / blue": about 33.1 V at 2.371 A.
- An **Arduino Nano** outside the board sets brightness (PWM), enables, and the current setpoint (I2C digipot).
- It is also meant to work as a **strobe**.
- Input: a **4S lithium battery**, 12-16.8 V. The input caps are 25 V parts, so 6S is not allowed.

### 1.2 The architecture (current; the August design in `ROOT\DESIGN_NOTES.md` is OBSOLETE)
A **single-inductor, multiple-output boost** with hysteretic, peak-current control built from discrete logic, plus
three linear current sinks.

**Power path:**
- Vin (J1, + J2 GND) -> **R1** (Bourns CSS4J-4026R-1L00F, 1 mOhm, 4-terminal shunt; **U25** INA241A4 makes the
  `Current` signal, 0.1 V/A) -> **L1** (Codaca CSCF3218-6R8MC, 6.8 uH, huge SMD, hand-fitted) -> node **LX**.
- **M1** (HYG180N10 TO-220, low-side) charges L1.
- Three back-to-back FET pairs route LX to the outputs:
  - M7 + M2 -> `Vout_1`, with `m2_source` the floating common source;
  - M6 + M3 -> `Vout_2` (`m3_source`);
  - M5 + M4 -> `Vout_3` (`m4_source`).
- Each `Vout_n` has its own cap bank: EEH-ZU / ZS 220 uF cans plus ceramics.
- Each LED goes from `Vout_n` (anode) to a **linear current sink**: a TLV9001 op-amp (U6 / U11 / U12 on the power
  board) drives **M10 / M9 / M8** (HYG180N10), with a 50 mOhm sense resistor (**R52 / R53 / R7**, WSL2512).
  - Output drains: J4 = Output1_drain, J7 = Output2_drain, J6 = Output3_drain.
  - Anodes: J3 = Vout_1, J8 = Vout_2, J5 = Vout_3.
- **D19** (5.0SMDJ54A TVS) clamps LX.
- **D28-D30** (Jingdao S2MW, LCSC C128729, SOD-123FL, bottom side) are pre-charge diodes Vin -> each Vout. They were
  added 2026-09-29 to kill a cold-power-up LX spike / L1 inrush.
- **Gate drivers:** UCC21520:
  - U19 for M1;
  - U15 for ch1 (M2 / M7);
  - U10 for ch2 (M3 / M6);
  - U8 for ch3 (M4 / M5).
  - Gate resistors: R15 10 ohm (M1); R13 / R23 / R47 220 ohm (rail FETs); R21 / R48 / R60 / R72 5.1 ohm.
- **Supplies:**
  - **U16** MC7812BD2TR4G (TO-263), the 12 V gate-drive rail, also sent to the Arduino end through J9.9;
  - **U17 / U1** L78L05: digital `5V` and `analog_5V`.

**Control (mostly on the control card):**
- An SR latch of cross-coupled NANDs (74HC00 U101-U103; 74HC14 U104 / U105). `Out` high = M1 on.
- **SET** fires when the inductor current is about 0 (`0A_hi`) and there is error demand, or when the low-error
  timeout expires.
- **RESET** fires when `Current` > `V_err` (peak current), blanked by `M1_ON`.
- Every SET clocks **U18** (CD4017B, one-hot counter), so the channel changes every switching cycle.
- **U28** (74HC4051 mux) puts that channel's error voltage on `V_err` (0.1 V/A scale).
- Per channel:
  - an error amp (TLV9001) compares `.1Vout_n` with `.1Vref_n`;
  - MCP6561 comparators (U2, U4, U5, U14, U21);
  - **U106** CD74HC4066 switches do the sample-and-hold of the references;
  - **U26** MCP4451-103 (quad 10k digipot, I2C, TSSOP-20, on the card's B.Cu) sets each channel's current reference.
    Code 0 = full current; defaults 86 / 103 / 103 give 2.635 / 2.371 / 2.371 A.
- `ena_out_n` = the LX-side FET enables. `M2_ON` / `out_2_on` / `out_3_on` = the rail-side enables.
- `0err_hi` = no demand. `VerrGT0` gates the enables.
- **U21** stops M1 when the 12 V rail is below about 9.8 V (UVLO-like).
- `ARD_M1_INHIBIT` (J9.11 -> D27) lets the Arduino hold M1 off.

## 2. The user, and how to work like the previous assistant

**Who:** the hardware designer. Experienced and precise. Reads everything, and catches wrong numbers. They want:
- **Decisions are theirs.** At every design decision (circuit or part change, copper or placement, accepting any
  regression, moving a fixed part, net renames, changing anything they drew or placed), stop and ask:
  - give the numbers;
  - give 2-3 options;
  - give a recommendation.
  - Ask everything you can **in one batch**.
  - Never decide silently. They have overruled recommendations before.
- **Pictures before anything is final.** Every new note, label or symbol, every moved symbol, and every new PCB part
  needs the user's OK on a picture. Look at every PNG you make; generating it is not checking it.
- **Schematic comments:** very brief (a few words, their style), right next to the part (nearly touching is fine),
  never stating the obvious. For new items on a sheet they arranged, show the spot and wait, or ask whether they want
  to place it themselves.
- **Plain, concrete language.** Short sentences; numbers with units and coordinates (x, y, layer). No jargon soup.
  When you report, say what is done, what is waiting for them, and what you could not verify.
- **Honesty about mistakes.** If you were wrong, say so plainly and fix it. If a command was interrupted, check what it
  actually did before saying it did nothing.
- **Their messages sometimes arrive as pasted drafts** written in another session. Do file-only work on them. Ask for
  an own-words OK before overwriting shipped files.
- **They work in KiCad / LTspice alongside you.** They may have files open and may save edits mid-task: they did so on
  2026-10-11 23:14 (see 5.3). Always hash-check before overwriting.
- **They own the git repo** (`ROOT` is a git repo, branch main; `BOOST-github` is a separate repo). Never commit or push
  unless asked.

   `ROOT\BOOST-github`.

   Tightening is fine.
   - Every saved board: DRC 0 errors, 0 unconnected, zones refilled, `--schematic-parity` 0.
   - The warning count stays at the baseline (power 49, card 1), or each new warning is explained and accepted.
   - ERC 0 on all three projects (main, power, card); sync_check 0.

4. **Never re-run a generator onto an existing file.** Every result gets a new name or a new folder. Check the output
   path does not exist. The user's latest file is only ever an input.
5. **Lock files:**
   - KiCad `~*.lck` beside a file means it may be open: ask before writing it.
   - Before writing a `.asc`, check that no LTspice process has it open (process command lines).
6. **KiCad Python `SaveBoard` drops the netclasses** from `.kicad_pro`. Copy the `.kicad_pro` and `.kicad_dru` back
   beside any Python-saved board before DRC.
   - Prefer `kicad-cli pcb drc --refill-zones --save-board` for refills; it does not touch the `.kicad_pro`.
   - Never `sed -i` a `.kicad_pro`.
   - Keep each file's own line endings: the boards are CRLF; the J9 library `.kicad_mod` is LF.
7. **Scripts:** write them with the Write tool; Bash heredocs mangle backslashes.
   - Never `rm` an unguarded variable (use `${VAR:?}`, `mktemp -d`, or move aside).
   - Never edit a bash script while it is running.
8. **IPC-2221 rises** on the power board: report them, with neck length. They are not pass/fail for necks under
   ~5 mm or for vias in plane-connected fields; flag regressions as decisions.
9. **Do not trust reviews blindly.** The files win; note disagreements in the report.
10. **Heavy jobs one at a time.**
    - DC solves: `WORKERS=3` while the user's apps hold ~20 GB (5 and 9 workers ran out of memory). One solve takes
      ~29 min.
    - At most 3-4 LTspice runs in parallel; watch the disk.
11. **Fixed parts** (do not move without asking):
    - J10 / J11 (the stack connector);
    - H1-H4, H5-H8 and all mounting holes, their keepouts and the board outlines / corner notch;
    - M1, M8, M9, M10 with their tab-screw holes and washer keepouts;
    - J1-J8 and J9;
    - the heights in `CLEARANCES.md` (lid over U18 / U28, washers under the card near D6, R94, D3, R84).

### 2.2 Working style that worked
- **One gated change per step.**
  - Each step is a new folder `RF\work\stepNN\`: a scratch KiCad copy, edit, refill, DRC / ERC / parity / sync, copper
    export, metrics.
  - Then `METRICS.md` / `CHANGELOG.md`, and before / after pictures.
  - Failed tries keep their folder with `FAILED.txt` / `SUPERSEDED.txt`.
- **What-ifs first.** Edit a copy of the copper-export JSON, re-solve, and only then edit a board.
- **Before replacing anything, hash-check it** against what you last wrote (`promotions.log`).
- **Report regressions with who accepted them.** Keep a "what I could not verify" list and a "where the review was
  wrong" list.
- **Give the user short status lines** during long work. They see "the user hasn't heard from you" prompts.

### 6.1 Known traps (each one has bitten before)
- **A via with no track or pad needs `(free yes)`**, or KiCad gives it a zone's net on save.
- **The card's GND is in the Power netclass:** 0.25 mm clearance. Default and Rail (5V) are 0.2.
- Card custom rules (`BOOST_control_RC2.kicad_dru`):
  - no foreign via within 2 mm of U2 In+ or Current copper (GND vias excepted);
  - via drill >= 0.3 mm;
  - NPTH hole clearance 0.3 mm.
- Power board: Vin vias need drill >= 0.4 mm; Vin tracks are never narrower than 1.0 mm.
- **A via or track end joins a pad only if one's centre lies inside the other.**
- **`remove_unused_layers`:** unflashed via layers are only a hole, which still needs 0.2 mm hole clearance.
- **J9's pads are flashed on all six layers**, so nothing routes between J9 pads on any layer.
- **Library vs board footprint:** editing a footprint on the board without its `.kicad_mod` adds a
  `lib_footprint_mismatch` warning (texts and courtyards are compared).
- **kicad-cli writes `.kicad_prl` files** next to boards and schematics it opens (netlist export, pos files). Work on
  copies.
- **After a few `BOARD.Remove` calls,** pcbnew's `GetTracks()` turns into bare SwigPyObjects: keep your own list,
  save, and reload.
- **Headless Edge from Python:** send stdout to a file and wait for the PNG.
- **Git Bash `grep -c $'\r'`** counts every line; count CRLF in Python.
- **The copper-edge vs H5 / H6 keep-out finding** (3.10-3.21 mm from the hole centre, inside the 3.5 mm circles; KiCad
  does not flag it) is harmless and known.
